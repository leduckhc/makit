Status & Activity — one record, two platforms

SPEC-status-and-activity · feat/status · toast + Activity for iOS and macOS

The problem this mockup exists to solve. The spec builds the right thing — a durable, copyable event record — and then, on the phone, hides it in Settings (D11: the home bar has five glass circles and a connection chip; a sixth overflowed by 4 px). So the surface whose entire purpose is answering “what just happened?” is two taps behind a gear, signalled by a dot that means “something, somewhere”. Everything below takes D6/D10 as settled and attacks that one weakness — plus the second-order question the spec leaves open: what container holds the list on each platform.

1 · The toast — the transient view

Both shells anchor top-right, max-width 380, surface-container-high, 3 px severity stripe, one-line mono detail preview, copy only when detail exists. The only per-shell value is the inset that clears the chrome parked at that edge: kToastInsetPhone 60 (below the floating glass bar) vs kToastInsetDesktop 40 (below the title strip). Both are drawn to scale here.

2 · The way in on iOS — four directions

The constraint is real: the bar is full. But “full bar” only rules out permanent chrome. Three of these four spend zero permanent pixels.

A · Status quo — a dot on the gear

FALLBACK

Settings › Activity, ActivityUnreadDot rides the gear

makit le-mbp
pi feat/status
pi · running
tap gear → Settings → scroll → Activity
two taps + a scan
  • Costs nothing. Ships today. Already implemented and tested.
  • The dot has no referent. A red dot on a gear says “settings changed”, not “a worktree failed 20 seconds ago”. The user must already know Activity exists to decode it.
  • Wrong depth for the use case. The whole feature is for the moment right after something happened. Two taps and a scroll is a research task, not a glance.
  • Settings is where you go to change things. Activity is something you read. Category mismatch — same reason Diagnostics-in-Settings is fine (you go there once) and this isn’t (you go there constantly).

Keep it as the always-available path — every list needs a stable address. Just don’t make it the only one.

  • Zero chrome, and the metaphor is already in the user’s hands. iOS trained everyone that the record of what the system told you lives behind the top edge. Toasts enter from that edge; pulling it down to find where they went is the same gesture, same direction, same mental model.
  • Scales to any number of events without touching the bar.
  • Invisible. No affordance, so discovery is accidental — and a feature nobody finds is worth exactly as much as the snackbar it replaced.
  • Gesture collision. The list beneath wants pull-to-refresh, and the glass bar is only 44 pt tall — the drag start zone overlaps the buttons on it.

Right instinct, wrong slot to bet on. Ship it later as a shortcut for people who already know, once something else has taught them the record exists.

  • Discoverable by construction. You don’t have to be told where the record is — you watched the toast turn into the door. That single animation teaches the entire feature, once, without a tooltip or an onboarding card.
  • Zero permanent chrome. No receipt exists when nothing has happened, so the 320 pt bar is never touched and D11’s overflow test stays green.
  • It answers complaint #2 literally. “It’s only a short notice” — now the notice leaves a mark in the place you were already looking.
  • A new persistent-ish element near the glass bar. Must stay visually subordinate: surface-container, no stripe, labelSmall, no glass — it is a bookmark, not a second bar.
  • Needs a decay rule or it becomes clutter. Proposal: the receipt lives while anything is unread, and fades after 90 s of no new events; the record and the gear dot persist regardless. It is an accelerator with a short memory, not a status bar.
  • Cheap. StatusToastLayer already owns the queue and statusBadgeProvider already publishes unread + worst — this is one more widget in a layer that is already mounted, no new route, no shell change.

Why this beats simply putting the bell on the bar. Even if the pixels existed, a permanent bell is a control that is empty most of the time — the phone pays rent on it every session for a thing it needs occasionally. The receipt inverts that: the affordance appears exactly when there is something to affordance, which is also the only moment it can be understood.

D · Fold it into the connection chip

REJECTED

one chip = connectivity + unread severity

makit le-mbp 5
two meanings, one tap target
  • Reuses chrome that already exists. No overflow, no new element.
  • Category error. Connectivity is continuous state (“am I connected right now?”); activity is discrete history (“what happened?”). One control cannot answer both — and when it turns red you can’t tell whether the server dropped or a worktree failed.
  • Steals the chip’s own tap target (it opens the server/device sheet today).
  • Green dot + red badge in one 22 pt pill is two competing severity signals 8 px apart.

  • You don’t lose your place. A pushed route replaces the screen; the reason you opened Activity is usually to make sense of something on that screen. The medium detent keeps both in view.
  • Dismissal is a flick, not a back button — and Activity is a read-and-leave surface, the single most sheet-shaped interaction iOS has.

Grouping. Rows gain a Just now / Earlier group head. With coalescing plus silent session posts, this list gets long fast, and “when” is the axis you scan by.

  • Why an inspector eventually wins on macOS. A modal dialog says “deal with me and leave”. During a 20-minute agent run you want the record parked — readable while you keep working, resizable, remembered between launches. That is a sidebar/inspector, and it is the native macOS shape for “secondary list about the thing in front of me”.
  • It also removes the copy/dismiss fight: no scrim, so text selection is just text selection.
  • Costs real layout work in the split view, and the dialog already passes its tests. Sequence it: ship the dialog with this slice, move to an inspector when the split view is next touched. The ActivityView body doesn’t change either way — that’s the whole point of having one list widget.
  • A third rendering (Activity as a pane in the tab-group system) is tempting — park it beside a session, keep it across launches — but panes are session-bound today, so it means a non-session pane concept. Genuinely interesting, clearly out of scope.

5 · Empty state — the one screen most users see most often

“Nothing has happened” should read as calm, not broken

makit le-mbp

Activity

Nothing to report
Outcomes, warnings and agent requests land here — and stay until you clear them.

The second line is doing real work. It is the only place the app ever explains that a durable record exists at all — worth its 12 pt, because a user who reads it once will look here the next time something flashes past. “and stay until you clear them” is the promise the snackbar broke.

Filtered-empty is a different state. If a severity filter is on and nothing matches, say “No failures” with a “Show all” action — not “Nothing to report”, which reads as “the feature is broken”.

6 · What I would change in the code

Deltas against what is on feat/status today

ordered by value per line changed

#ChangeWhereWhy
1 Add the receipt pill status/activity_receipt.dart (new), mounted in StatusToastLayer Makes the record discoverable without spending a pixel of the 320 pt bar. Reads statusBadgeProvider, which already publishes unread+worst. Fades 90 s after the last event.
2 Activity as a detented sheet on iOS status/activity_screen.dart → showModalBottomSheet; keep the route for Settings You open Activity about the screen you are on. A pushed route throws that context away. Same ActivityView body, so its tests stand.
3 Anchor the desktop toast in the pane column desktop/desktop_app.dart — mount the layer inside the pane, not the window Right-aligned over the sidebar is 200 px from the thing that spoke, and in a split it hides which pane spoke.
4 Just now / Earlier group heads status/activity_view.dart Silent session posts (D7) make this list long. Time is the scan axis, and the group head is cheaper than a per-row absolute timestamp.
5 Distinguish filtered-empty from empty status/activity_view.dart — _EmptyActivity “Nothing to report” under an active Failures filter reads as a bug.
6 Inspector rail on macOS split view — follow-up, not this slice A record you read during a 20-minute run wants to be parked, not modal. Deferred because the dialog works and this touches layout.

What I did not touch. D6 (top anchoring, not glass), D5 (dwell + max 3), D4 (8 s coalescing), D9 (no persistence), D10 (insets, contrast) — all correct, and drawing them only confirmed it. The single weak point was the way in on the phone, and it turned out not to need a bigger bar; it needed the toast to stop disappearing without a trace.
